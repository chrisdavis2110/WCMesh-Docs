## Repeater settings updates

#### txdelay
This has been an ongoing discussion, even having fellow meshers create multiple [whitepapers](../whitepapers.md) on the topic. With the latest whitepaper, and with some talk on the zoom, the txdelay setting needs to be reworked. The initial idea was to have higher elevation nodes have a longer delay while more local nodes have a shorter delay. The new idea is having higher elevation nodes with strong long haul links have the shorter delay so that packets to spread faster along the mesh.

#### loop.detect
Loops are still an issue and we are still seeing messages be repeated from hours before with longer paths than the original, having being caught in a loop. Our current recommendation is set loop.detect to moderate, but we may need to use strict to eliminte loops. Best thing is everyone switch to multibyte for better diagnosis of where the loops are happening.

| State	| 1 byte | 2 byte | 3 byte |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| minimal | ≥ 4 | ≥ 2 | ≥1 |
| moderate | ≥ 2 | ≥ 1 | ≥ 1 |
| strict | ≥ 1 | ≥ 1 | ≥ 1 |


## Regions/Scopes
Recap from the previous State of the Mesh:

Since we are on our own frequency, we pretty much have a built in region setting. However, bridges to Central Valley and Central Coast have been in the talks for a while. Both sides have slowly been creeping down towards SoCal. A lot of folks would like to see a global mesh, but I believe that local is better. Other states have linked with nearby states and while they like the connection, they don't like eveything that comes with it: adverts, wardrive pings, etc. Once two meshes merge, they quickly realize that they can't handle the additional traffic and have to implement features they aren't completely ready for. Personally, I would like to keep things local and not implement bridges. We could add regions to our repeaters if we want to really define our mesh. There still is no way of decoding scopes in the companion software so everyone will still see every message, even if it is scoped. It is just that a repeater will drop a packet if the scope a message carries is not defined as a region on the repeater.

The other California meshes have been discussing implementing regions and have been trying to come up with an all California tree. I have complied them onto this map: [https://regions.wcmesh.com](https://regions.wcmesh.com). You can use this [website](https://matkam.github.io/meshcore-settings/#socal) to help set regions on your repeater.

The main thing for us right now is to start adding basic regions, mostly just #socal and your local county. A breakdown of how regions and scopes work and how to implement them on your repeater is [here](../regions/index.md). This will help reduce the flood traffic across the whole mesh, keeing some local channels local. Public will always be open to the whole mesh. Create local channels and add scopes to the channels that match local repeaters' regions.
